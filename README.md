

PROCESS PLANNING AND SIMULATION OF A FIBRE PARTS MANUFACTURING PLANT USING DELMIA


1. AIM

To develop the process plan and simulate the plant layout for the batch production of fibre parts using cutting, profiling, machining, and material-handling equipment, and to create a virtual manufacturing environment using DELMIA for studying material flow, machine arrangement, storage, productivity, quality control, and workplace safety.


2. OBJECTIVES

1. To study the manufacturing process of fibre parts.
2. To select a suitable fibre material for component manufacturing.
3. To prepare a suitable process sequence for batch production.
4. To identify the machines, equipment, storage areas, and inspection facilities required.
5. To estimate the approximate manufacturing cycle time.
6. To simulate the plant layout using DELMIA Plant Layout Design.
7. To study the material flow between different manufacturing stations.
8. To minimize unnecessary material movement and handling.
9. To incorporate storage areas, operator areas, conveyors, and safety fencing in the virtual plant.
10. To visualize the complete fibre-parts manufacturing facility in a 3D simulation environment.


3. INTRODUCTION

Fibre parts are lightweight composite components manufactured from fibre-reinforced materials and are used in a wide range of automotive, electrical, industrial, and general engineering applications.

In this work, a virtual manufacturing plant for fibre-part production is considered. The manufacturing system consists of raw-material receiving and storage, material inspection, cutting, profile machining, drilling, edge trimming, deburring, cleaning, final inspection, finished-parts storage, packing, and dispatch.

The plant layout is simulated using DELMIA Plant Layout Design. The simulation includes machines, raw-material tables, conveyors, storage racks, material-handling equipment, inspection areas, finished-goods storage, loading and unloading areas, workers, and safety fencing.

The DELMIA simulation provides a 3D virtual representation of the manufacturing facility and helps in visualizing the arrangement of machines, material flow, storage areas, material-handling systems, and safety zones before physical implementation.

7. MANUFACTURING OPERATIONS


7.1 RAW MATERIAL RECEIVING

Fibre sheets, plates, or preformed fibre materials are received from suppliers.

The incoming material is checked for visible damage, dimensional condition, and general quality before being transferred to the storage area.


7.2 RAW MATERIAL STORAGE

The fibre materials are stored on designated raw-material tables, racks, or storage systems.

The storage area is positioned near the cutting section to reduce unnecessary material transportation.


7.3 MATERIAL INSPECTION

The raw material is inspected before processing to ensure that the fibre sheet or plate is suitable for further manufacturing.

The material is checked for:

• Surface defects
• Cracks or damage
• Thickness
• Overall dimensions
• Visual quality


7.4 CUTTING TO REQUIRED SIZE

The fibre sheet or plate is cut into the required dimensions according to the component design.

The cutting operation prepares the material for subsequent profile machining.

Machine Used: Cutting / Machining Machine


7.5 PROFILE CUTTING / MACHINING

The cut fibre material is machined to obtain the required component profile.

This operation produces the required:

• Outer profile
• Curves
• Slots
• Contours
• Component geometry

Machine Used: Profile Machine / CNC or Suitable Machining Equipment


7.6 DRILLING / HOLE MAKING

Required holes are produced according to the component design.

Hole diameter, position, and alignment are controlled during the operation.

Machine Used: Drilling Machine


7.7 EDGE TRIMMING AND FINISHING

The edges of the fibre component are trimmed and finished to obtain the required dimensions and surface condition.

Sharp or irregular edges are removed to improve component quality and handling safety.


7.8 DEBURRING AND CLEANING

After machining, burrs, loose fibres, and machining residues are removed.

The component is cleaned before being transferred to the final inspection area.


7.9 FINAL INSPECTION

The finished fibre component is inspected for dimensional accuracy, profile accuracy, hole position, surface quality, and visible defects.

Components that meet the specified requirements are transferred to finished-parts storage.


7.10 FINISHED PARTS STORAGE

The inspected components are transferred to the finished-parts storage area.

The parts are arranged on storage racks or suitable containers to prevent damage and maintain organized inventory.


7.11 PACKING AND DISPATCH

The finished fibre parts are packed using suitable packaging materials and transferred to the dispatch area.

The products are then loaded for transportation to the required destination.

12.8 INSPECTION AREA

An inspection table/workstation is provided for checking the finished fibre components.

12.9 FINISHED PARTS STORAGE

The finished and inspected fibre components are stored in designated storage racks or suitable containers before packing and dispatch.


12.10 MATERIAL HANDLING AREA

Material-handling equipment such as trolleys or forklifts is represented in the virtual plant for movement of raw materials and finished components.
